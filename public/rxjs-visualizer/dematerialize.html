dematerialize

Turns a stream of Notification objects back into the events they describe: a next notification emits its value, a complete notification completes.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, one frame per millisecond

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
merge(
  at(2, Notification.createNext('x')), at(5, Notification.createNext('y')),
  at(8, Notification.createNext('z')), at(11, Notification.createComplete()), end(13),
).pipe(dematerialize())

3 essential criteria

the source values
notifications: three nexts carrying x, y, z, then a complete notification at 11
a next notification
is emitted as its value, in the same frame
a complete notification
completes the output at once and unsubscribes the source, whose own completion at 13 is never seen

Resulting sink

x at 2, y at 5, z at 8, complete at 11

Workflow

  1. The notifications at 2, 5 and 8 are nexts: x, y and z pass through as plain values.
  2. The notification at 11 is a complete: the output completes there and the subscription to the source ends.
  3. The source goes on to complete at 13, but nobody is listening any more.

In the spec

The source lane shows its values as {x}, {y}, {z} and |, the strings the test maps to notifications; the output lane ends at 11 under the | marble, two frames before the source lane completes, and the subscription bar ends there too.
