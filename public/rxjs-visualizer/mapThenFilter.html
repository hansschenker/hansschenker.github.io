map → filter

Two operators in one pipe: map multiplies each value by 10, then filter keeps only the results above 10.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, one frame per millisecond

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
merge(at(1, 1), at(3, 2), at(5, 3), end(7))
  .pipe(map(n => n * 10), filter(n => n > 10))

3 essential criteria

the stages
map then filter; the output of one is the source of the next, drawn as its own lane
what map does
1, 2, 3 become 10, 20, 30 in the frame they arrive
what filter does
keeps a value only when it is above 10, so 10 is dropped and 20, 30 pass

Resulting sink

20 at 3, 30 at 5, complete at 7

Workflow

  1. 1 at 1 becomes 10 on the map lane; filter drops it, so the last lane stays empty at 1.
  2. 2 at 3 becomes 20 and passes; 3 at 5 becomes 30 and passes.
  3. The source completes at 7 and the completion runs through both stages in that frame.

In the spec

Read one column top to bottom: e1, the map row, its output lane, the filter row, the expected vs actual lane. Column 1 has a marble on the map lane and nothing below it; columns 3 and 5 have one on every lane.
