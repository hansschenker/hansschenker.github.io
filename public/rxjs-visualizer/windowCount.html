windowCount

Cuts the source into consecutive windows of the given number of values, emitting each window as an observable of its own the moment it opens.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, with each window flattened as number:value

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
merge(at(3, 'a'), at(7, 'b'), at(11, 'c'), at(15, 'd'), at(19, 'e'), at(23, 'f'), at(27, 'g'), at(31, 'h'), at(35, 'i'), end(39))
  .pipe(windowCount(3), mergeMap((window, i) => window.pipe(map(v => (i + 1) + ':' + v))))

4 essential criteria

the window size
3: a window completes with its third value
the first window
opens at subscription, frame 0, before any value
the next window
opens on the very frame the third value closes the one before: y at 11 with c, z at 23 with f, w at 35 with i
on completion
the open window completes with the source: w at 39, empty, having seen no value

Resulting sink

1:a at 3, 1:b at 7, 1:c at 11, 2:d at 15, 2:e at 19, 2:f at 23, 3:g at 27, 3:h at 31, 3:i at 35, complete at 39

Workflow

  1. Subscribing at 0 opens window 1 at once; a at 3 and b at 7 go into it, and c at 11 is its third value: window 1 completes and window 2 opens on that frame.
  2. d, e and f fill window 2 at 15, 19 and 23; f closes it and opens window 3, which g, h and i fill at 27, 31 and 35.
  3. i closes window 3 and opens window 4 at 35; the source completes at 39, so window 4 completes empty and the output completes with it.

In the spec

The output lane has four marbles, x, y, z and w, and under it four rows: the first hangs from frame 0, and every next one hangs from the frame the previous row ends in, so the bars touch end to start. The last row is a bar with nothing on it but its completion.
