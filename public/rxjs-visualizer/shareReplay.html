shareReplay

Multicasts like share and replays the last n values to every late subscriber.

Sample: the drawn spec as code: the same three subscribers, a replay of one

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
const shared = merge(at(1, 1), at(3, 2), at(5, 3), at(10, 4), end(12)).pipe(shareReplay(1));
shared.subscribe((v) => log('s1', v));
timer(4).subscribe(() => shared.subscribe((v) => log('s2', v)));
timer(8).subscribe(() => shared.subscribe((v) => log('s3', v)));

3 essential criteria

buffer size
1: the latest value is kept
a late subscriber
receives the kept value on the frame it joins, then the live ones: s2 gets 2 at 4, s3 gets 3 at 8
source subscriptions
one, as with share

Resulting sink

s1 1 at 1, s1 2 at 3, s2 2 at 4, s1 3 at 5, s2 3 at 5, s3 3 at 8, s1 4 at 10, s2 4 at 10, s3 4 at 10

Workflow

  1. s1 subscribes at 0; 1 at 1 and 2 at 3 reach s1, and 2 is kept.
  2. s2 joins at 4 and receives the kept 2 at once; 3 at 5 reaches s1 and s2 and is kept.
  3. s3 joins at 8 and receives the kept 3 at once; 4 at 10 reaches all three, and the completion at 12.

In the spec

Subscriber 2 joins at 4 and gets the buffered 2 on that very frame, then 3 live; subscriber 3 at 8 gets the buffered 3; the source is still subscribed once.
