zipWith

Pairs the values of the inputs by position: the first with the first, the second with the second.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, one frame per millisecond

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
const e2 = merge(at(6, 'd'), at(11, 'e'), at(16, 'f'), end(25));
const e3 = merge(at(8, 'h'), at(13, 'i'), at(18, 'j'), NEVER);
merge(at(3, 'a'), at(6, 'b'), at(9, 'c'), end(12))
  .pipe(zipWith(e2, e3))

3 essential criteria

pairing
by position: the first values of e1, e2, e3 together, then the seconds, then the thirds
each tuple
waits for the slowest input, e3, at 8, 13 and 18
completion
when an input has completed with its buffer empty: e1 completed at 12, its c paired at 18

Resulting sink

[a, d, h] at 8, [b, e, i] at 13, [c, f, j] at 18, complete at 18

Workflow

  1. a at 3 and d at 6 wait for h at 8: [a, d, h].
  2. b at 6, e at 11, i at 13: [b, e, i] at 13. c at 9 waits for f at 16 and j at 18.
  3. j at 18 completes [c, f, j]; e1 completed at 12 and has nothing left, so the output completes at 18 although e2 and e3 go on.

In the spec

e1 completes at 12 while its c is still waiting; each pill appears on the frame of the last value it needs. At 18 the output completes and the subscription bars of e2 and e3 end, although e2 runs to 25 and e3 never completes.
