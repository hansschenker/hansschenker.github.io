skipWhile

Drops values while the predicate holds; once it fails, everything passes without being checked again.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, counting the predicate calls

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
let calls = 0;
merge(at(2, 'c'), at(5, 'd'), at(8, 'e'), at(11, 'f'), at(14, 'g'), at(17, 'h'), end(20))
  .pipe(skipWhile((v) => { calls++; return v !== 'e'; }))
  .subscribe({ next: log, complete: () => log('predicate called', calls, 'times') });

3 essential criteria

predicate
v !== 'e', the spec's predicate
the first failing value
e at 8 is emitted and switches the predicate off
later values
pass unchecked: the predicate ran 3 times, for c, d and e

Resulting sink

e at 8, f at 11, g at 14, h at 17, predicate called 3 times at 20

Workflow

  1. c at 2 and d at 5 satisfy the predicate and are dropped.
  2. e at 8 fails it and passes; the predicate is never called again.
  3. f, g, h pass at 11, 14, 17 without a call; the completion at 20 passes.

In the spec

The operator row counts the predicate's calls and drops a line from each value it ran on: c, d and e; f, g and h have none. a and b sit left of frame 0, before the subscription.
