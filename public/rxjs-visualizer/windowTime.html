windowTime

Cuts the source into consecutive windows of a fixed duration, on a clock that starts at subscription, each window an observable of the values that fell into it.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, each window flattened as index:value

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
merge(at(3, 'a'), at(6, 'b'), at(9, 'c'), at(12, 'd'), at(15, 'e'), at(18, 'f'), at(21, 'g'), at(24, 'h'), end(27))
  .pipe(windowTime(10), mergeMap((w, i) => w.pipe(map((v) => i + ':' + v))))

3 essential criteria

the window span
10 frames, the spec's t; windows open at 0, 10 and 20 whether or not a value arrived
a window
is emitted on the output the frame it opens, as an observable; its values follow on the window itself
the last window
is cut by the source completion at 27, before its 10 frames are up

Resulting sink

0:a at 3, 0:b at 6, 0:c at 9, 1:d at 12, 1:e at 15, 1:f at 18, 2:g at 21, 2:h at 24, complete at 27

Workflow

  1. Window 0 opens at the subscription and takes a, b, c; it closes at 10, where window 1 opens.
  2. Window 1 takes d, e, f and closes at 20; window 2 opens and takes g, h.
  3. The source completes at 27: window 2 completes with it, and so does the output.

In the spec

The operator row has three clock windows end to end, the last cut at 27; the output lane has one marble per window at 0, 10 and 20, and under it three rows, each with its window’s values and the expected cold as rings. The 1 and 2 before the caret sit left of the ruler, outside the subscription.
