exhaustAll

Subscribes the inner observable the source emits and ignores every further inner until it completes; the next one to arrive after that is subscribed.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, one frame per millisecond

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
const x = merge(at(2, 'a'), at(6, 'b'), at(10, 'c'), end(13));
const y = merge(at(3, 'd'), at(6, 'e'), at(10, 'f'), end(14));
const z = merge(at(3, 'g'), at(6, 'h'), at(10, 'i'), end(14));
merge(at(6, x), at(14, y), at(20, z), end(34))
  .pipe(exhaustAll())

3 essential criteria

source values
observables: x at 6, y at 14, z at 20
the rule
while an inner runs, newer inners are dropped: y at 14 is never subscribed, x runs to 19
after it completes
z at 20 finds nothing running and is subscribed; the output completes with the source at 34

Resulting sink

a at 8, b at 12, c at 16, g at 23, h at 26, i at 30, complete at 34

Workflow

  1. x arrives at 6 and is subscribed: a, b, c at 8, 12, 16, complete at 19.
  2. y arrives at 14 while x runs: dropped, never subscribed; d, e, f are never emitted.
  3. z arrives at 20 after x completed and is subscribed: g, h, i at 23, 26, 30; the source completes at 34 and so does the output.

In the spec

Two inner rows for three source values: y at 14 is crossed out on the operator row and has no row, while the rows for x and z do not overlap.
