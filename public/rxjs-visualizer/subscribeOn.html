subscribeOn

Delays the subscription itself, on the given scheduler; once subscribed, nothing else is delayed.

Sample: the drawn spec as code: a hot source, subscribed 3 frames late

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
const hot = new Subject();
timer(2).subscribe(() => hot.next('a'));
timer(5).subscribe(() => hot.next('b'));
timer(8).subscribe(() => hot.complete());
hot.pipe(subscribeOn(asyncScheduler, 3)).subscribe({ next: log, complete: () => log('complete') });

3 essential criteria

the delay
3 frames before the source is subscribed, the spec's t
a hot source
keeps emitting meanwhile: a at 2 fires with nobody listening and is lost
after that
b at 5 and the completion at 8 pass through in their own frames

Resulting sink

b at 5, complete at 8

Workflow

  1. The output is subscribed at 0, but the subscription to the source is scheduled for 3.
  2. a fires at 2 on the hot source and is missed.
  3. The source is subscribed at 3; b at 5 and the completion at 8 are delivered unchanged.

In the spec

The subscription bar of e1 starts at 3, not at 0, and the single window on the operator row spans exactly that gap; the a marble sits left of the bar’s start and has no counterpart on the output lane.
