publishLast

Multicasts the source through an AsyncSubject: nothing is passed on until the source completes, then every subscriber gets its last value and completes.

Sample: the drawn spec as code: three subscribers at 0, 4 and 8, connected at 0

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
const shared = merge(at(1, 1), at(3, 2), at(5, 3), at(10, 4), end(12)).pipe(publishLast());
shared.subscribe((v) => log('s1', v));
timer(4).subscribe(() => shared.subscribe((v) => log('s2', v)));
timer(8).subscribe(() => shared.subscribe((v) => log('s3', v)));
shared.connect();

3 essential criteria

while the source runs
1, 2, 3 and 4 reach nobody; only the last is kept
at the completion
4 is delivered to every subscriber at 12, whenever it joined, and they complete
the connection
one subscription of the source at 0, through connect()

Resulting sink

s1 4 at 12, s2 4 at 12, s3 4 at 12

Workflow

  1. s1, s2 and s3 join at 0, 4 and 8 and wait; the values at 1, 3, 5 and 10 are swallowed.
  2. The source completes at 12: 4 and the completion go to all three in that frame.

In the spec

The three subscriber lanes are empty until the column of the source completion, where each has a 4 marble and the completion bar together.
