debounce

Emits a value only once the duration observable made for it fires; a newer value discards the older one and its duration.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, one frame per millisecond

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
merge(at(1, 'a'), at(6, 'b'), at(7, 'c'), at(12, 'd'), at(14, 'e'), at(15, 'f'), end(20))
  .pipe(debounce(() => timer(3)))

3 essential criteria

the duration
a fresh observable per value; here it fires after 3 frames, like the cold e2
a newer value
unsubscribes the running duration and starts its own; the older value is never emitted
what is emitted
the value whose duration fired, at that frame

Resulting sink

a at 4, c at 10, f at 18, complete at 20

Workflow

  1. a at 1 starts a duration; it fires at 4 and a is emitted.
  2. b at 6 starts one, c at 7 cuts it and starts its own; c is emitted at 10.
  3. d at 12, e at 14 and f at 15 each cut the one before; only f reaches its firing, at 18. The source completes at 20.

In the spec

Six inner rows, one per value: the rows of b, d and e are cut short before their x, the rows of a, c and f reach it and drop the value onto the output lane.
