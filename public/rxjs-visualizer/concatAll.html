concatAll

Subscribes to the inner observables the source emits one at a time, each after the previous completes.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, one frame per millisecond

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
const x = merge(at(4, 'a'), at(11, 'b'), end(18));
const y = merge(at(3, 'c'), at(5, 'd'), end(9));
const z = merge(at(3, 'e'), at(6, 'f'), end(8));
merge(at(1, x), at(5, y), at(10, z), end(17))
  .pipe(concatAll())

3 essential criteria

source values
observables: x at 1, y at 5, z at 10; concatAll is concatMap with an identity project
concurrency
one: y and z wait in a queue until the inner before them completes
order
the order they arrived in: x, then y from 19, then z from 28

Resulting sink

a at 5, b at 12, c at 22, d at 24, e at 31, f at 34, complete at 36

Workflow

  1. x arrives at 1 and is subscribed: a at 5, b at 12, complete at 19.
  2. y at 5 and z at 10 arrive while x runs and are queued; y is subscribed at 19: c at 22, d at 24, complete at 28.
  3. z is subscribed at 28: e at 31, f at 34, complete at 36; the output completes with it, long after the source at 17.

In the spec

The rows of y and z show dotted queued spans, y's from 5 to 19 when x completes, z's from 10 to 28 when y completes; their marbles are drawn from their subscription frames.
