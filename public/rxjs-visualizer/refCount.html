refCount

Connects a published (connectable) source when its first subscriber arrives and disconnects it when the last one leaves, counting them in between.

Sample: the drawn spec as code, one frame per millisecond

// at(ms, v): v at ms; end(ms): complete at ms; 1 frame = 1 ms
const at = (ms, v) => timer(ms).pipe(map(() => v));
const end = (ms) => timer(ms).pipe(ignoreElements());
merge(at(2, '1'), at(4, '2'), at(8, '3'), at(10, '4'), at(13, '5'), end(15))
  .pipe(publish(), refCount())

3 essential criteria

publish()
makes the source connectable: nothing is subscribed until connect() is called
the first subscriber
takes the count from 0 to 1 and refCount calls connect(): the cold is subscribed at 0
the last to leave
takes the count back to 0 and the connection is unsubscribed; here the completion at 15 ends it for the only subscriber

Resulting sink

1 at 2, 2 at 4, 3 at 8, 4 at 10, 5 at 13, complete at 15

Workflow

  1. Subscribing at 0 is the first subscription: the count becomes 1 and refCount connects, so the cold source is subscribed at 0.
  2. 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 pass through the connection at 2, 4, 8, 10 and 13.
  3. The completion at 15 reaches the subscriber and tears the connection down: the count is 0 again.

In the spec

Watch the subscription bar of e1: it starts at 0, the frame the output was subscribed, because refCount connected there; publish() alone would never have subscribed it. The counting itself is drawn in refCountMany.
